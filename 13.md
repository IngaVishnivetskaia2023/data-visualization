## Propotion and Scale

#### Understand Orientation: 
Choose the chart's orientation based on how you want viewers to read the information. 
Landscape orientation typically guides the viewer's gaze from left to right, while vertical can lead from top to bottom. 
Make sure the orientation aligns with the flow of the data.

#### Determine Size Ratio: 
Use **the golden ratio** (approximately `1:1.618`) as a starting point for sizing your charts. This helps achieve a visually pleasing balance. 
Depending on your design and the number of charts, adjust the size accordingly—be cautious of overcrowding if you have multiple charts.

### A sequential color palettes 
is composed of varying intensities of a single hue of color at uniform saturation. 
Variability in luminance of adjacent colors corresponds to the variation in data values that they are used to render.

### A divergent color palettes 
is made of two sequential color palettes (each of a different hue) stacked next to each other with an inflection point in the middle. 
These become helpful when visualizing data with variations in two different directions.
